Hallucination Detection in Large Language Models

Hallucination refers to the generation of content that is fluent and plausible-sounding but
not grounded in truth or in the provided source material. Several techniques have been
proposed to detect hallucination in LLM outputs.

SelfCheckGPT, introduced by Manakul et al. in 2023, is a sampling-based approach to detect
hallucination without requiring access to external resources or the model's internal token
probabilities. The core idea is that if a language model has genuine knowledge of a given
concept, sampled responses to the same question are likely to be similar and consistent.
Conversely, if the model is hallucinating, sampled responses are likely to diverge and
contain inconsistent facts. SelfCheckGPT measures this by generating multiple stochastic
samples of an answer and computing consistency scores such as average pairwise similarity,
BERTScore, or NLI-based agreement between the samples.

Natural Language Inference (NLI) models can also be used for factual consistency checking.
Given a claim and a piece of evidence text, an NLI model classifies the relationship as
entailment (the evidence supports the claim), contradiction (the evidence contradicts the
claim), or neutral (the evidence neither supports nor contradicts the claim). This approach
can be applied claim-by-claim to a generated answer, checking each claim against retrieved
evidence chunks to estimate what fraction of the answer is actually supported by the source
material.
